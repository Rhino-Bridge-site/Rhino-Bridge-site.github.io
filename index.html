Rhino Bridge.

Rhino Bridge is a self-custody application that aggregates many separate bridge paths into one interface, scanning routes between Ethereum and a wide set of other chains and selecting whichever one delivers the most value to your destination address. The project was historically built on StarkEx, StarkWare's Layer-2 scaling engine, which gave early versions of the app a cheap, verifiable settlement path before the aggregator expanded to route across additional networks and bridges. Rhino Bridge never takes custody of what you send: an amount leaves your wallet on the source chain and the matching value lands in a wallet you control on the destination chain, with the route, fee and arrival estimate shown before you confirm.

How the estimate is calculated

Fee = 0.25 units + 0.3% of the amount, rounded to two decimal places and capped at the amount sent. The remaining amount uses the same units, at a 1:1 conversion. Time starts at 2 minutes, adds 1 minute per 2,500 units or part thereof, and is capped at 12 minutes. The displayed range adds 2 minutes to that estimate.

What Rhino Bridge does

Rhino Bridge is an aggregator: instead of running one fixed path between two networks, it checks several underlying bridges and liquidity routes each time you enter a transfer and quotes the single path that gets the most value across. You keep custody throughout — the app never holds your assets in a pooled account. It constructs a transaction you sign from your own wallet, the source chain settles that transaction, and the destination chain credits your address once the chosen route has finalized. The interface is the same regardless of which underlying path gets selected, so the comparison work that would otherwise mean checking several bridge sites yourself happens automatically; the mechanics behind that selection are laid out in the Rhino.fi docs.

Why an aggregator beats a single fixed route

A bridge that only operates one path has no alternative when that path is congested, temporarily expensive, or missing a chain pair you need. Rhino Bridge instead evaluates several routes against each other for a given transfer and surfaces the one with the best net outcome at that moment, which matters because relative costs between routes shift as networks get busy or liquidity moves. The trade-off is that the exact path your funds travel can differ between two transfers of the same size made minutes apart.

Rhino Bridge's roots in StarkEx

Early versions of Rhino Bridge settled through StarkEx, the validity-proof scaling engine StarkWare built for exchanges and payment applications, which batches many transfers into a single proof verified on Ethereum. That heritage is why the project still describes itself as built on StarkEx scaling technology even as its current routing covers destinations well beyond the original StarkEx application; you can read how that proving system is tracked independently on L2BEAT.

Chains Rhino Bridge connects

Rhino Bridge's routing table spans Ethereum mainnet alongside a growing set of rollups and sidechains, and which pairs are available changes as the aggregator adds support. The practical approach is to check the live chain list inside the app for your specific pair rather than assume a route exists, since an aggregator's value comes precisely from covering many destinations rather than specializing in one.

Ethereum as the common anchor

Ethereum remains the reference chain most Rhino Bridge routes connect back to, both because it is where the largest pool of liquidity and stablecoins sits and because many of the destination rollups ultimately settle their own state back to Ethereum. Background on how rollups relate to the base chain they settle to is covered on ethereum.org's layer-2 page.

Rhino Bridge fees and timing

A Rhino Bridge transfer carries the network fee for the signed transaction on the source chain plus whatever the selected route charges for moving value across; because the route can change transfer to transfer, the fee is quoted at the moment you enter an amount rather than published as one fixed number. Timing follows the chain pair: a transfer between two fast rollups can land in minutes, while a route that touches a slower-finalizing chain takes longer. Reading the quote before you sign

Rhino Bridge shows the amount that will actually arrive, after fees, alongside the amount you are sending — that figure is the one worth judging a transfer by, not the headline rate, since the gap between the two is where routing cost and network fee both show up. You can verify what landed afterward by checking the destination address on a block explorer such as Etherscan when the destination is Ethereum or an Ethereum-settled chain.

Bridging with Rhino Bridge step by step

The sequence stays the same across chain pairs, which is what makes repeat transfers through Rhino Bridge predictable once you have done it once; it starts with a self-custody wallet you already control, not an account created inside the app.
  • Open the app and link a self-custody wallet holding the asset you want to move.
  • Pick the source chain and the destination chain for the transfer.
  • Enter the amount; Rhino Bridge selects a route and quotes the fee and arrival amount.
  • Approve the token if needed, then sign the transaction from your wallet.
  • Wait for the source chain to confirm; the destination balance appears once the route finalizes.
  • Check the destination wallet or a block explorer to confirm the credited amount.

Why two transfers of the same size can differ

Because routing is dynamic, sending the same amount on the same pair an hour apart can produce a slightly different fee or arrival time if network conditions or available liquidity on the underlying paths have shifted. This is expected behavior for an aggregator and is not a sign anything went wrong.

Is Rhino Bridge safe to use

Rhino Bridge's self-custody design means the application itself never holds a balance of your assets between transfers; each transfer is a transaction you sign that settles on-chain through whichever route was selected. The risks that remain are the ones present in any self-custody transfer: confirming you are on the genuine app, reading what a signature authorizes, and managing token approvals sensibly.

Approvals worth paying attention to

When a transfer involves an ERC-20 token, you will typically grant the bridge contract permission to move a specific amount. Reviewing that allowance before signing, and revoking it later if you no longer plan to use that route, keeps the set of standing permissions your wallet carries small.

Recovery phrases stay with you, period

A legitimate Rhino Bridge flow only ever asks for a transaction signature inside your own wallet software. Any message, pop-up or support contact asking you to enter your recovery phrase anywhere is impersonating the service, full stop, and should be closed without a second thought.

Rhino Bridge vs a single-route bridge

A bridge built around one canonical path, such as a rollup's own official bridge, is the reference route for that specific chain pair and is often the simplest option when you already know exactly where you are headed. Rhino Bridge instead sits a layer above that choice: it evaluates several of those canonical and third-party paths together and picks whichever suits the transfer you are making right now, which is more useful when you are not sure which underlying bridge currently offers the better terms, or when your destination chain changes from one transfer to the next. Neither approach is strictly better; a single-route bridge can be worth checking directly for very large transfers where comparing the native path against the aggregated quote is worth the extra step, and the full set of routes Rhino Bridge currently evaluates is kept current in Rhino.fi's own documentation.

After you bridge: what Rhino Bridge enables

Once a transfer through Rhino Bridge lands, the asset behaves like any native holding on its destination chain — it can be used in a DEX, supplied to a lending market, or held until you are ready to move it again. Because the app covers many chains from one interface, it also works well as the step before consolidating holdings scattered across several networks back onto one chain, or before spreading a position out across a few chains for separate strategies. Whichever direction you are moving in, the same route-selection step runs each time, so the process of getting an asset where it needs to be stays the same regardless of how many hops the underlying path actually takes.
What is Rhino Bridge?
Rhino Bridge is a self-custody application that aggregates several bridge routes between Ethereum and other chains, selecting whichever path delivers the most value for a given transfer rather than relying on one fixed route.
Is Rhino Bridge built on StarkEx?
Rhino Bridge was historically built on StarkEx, StarkWare's Layer-2 scaling engine, and still describes that heritage even though its current routing now covers destinations well beyond the original StarkEx application.
Does Rhino Bridge ever hold my funds?
No. Rhino Bridge is self-custody: each transfer is a transaction you sign from your own wallet, and the app does not hold a pooled balance of user assets between transfers.
Why does the fee for a Rhino Bridge transfer change?
Because Rhino Bridge selects from several underlying routes, the fee reflects whichever path is quoted as best at that moment. Network conditions and available liquidity on those routes shift over time, so the same transfer repeated later can quote differently.
How long does a Rhino Bridge transfer take?
It depends on the chain pair and the route selected. A transfer between two fast rollups can land within minutes, while a pair involving a slower-finalizing chain takes longer.
Which chains can I use with Rhino Bridge?
Rhino Bridge connects Ethereum to a growing set of rollups and sidechains. The exact list of supported pairs changes as the aggregator adds routes, so check the live chain list inside the app for your specific transfer.
Can Rhino Bridge move an asset back to Ethereum?
Yes. The same aggregated routing works in either direction, selecting a path back to Ethereum the same way it selects a path away from it.
